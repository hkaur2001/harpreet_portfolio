# Enough — Product Brief

## Vision

Make spontaneous time with friends easier to initiate by separating **interest** from **commitment**.

A casual plan should not require someone to act like an event organizer before they know whether the plan is worth organizing. Enough gives people a low-pressure way to say: **“I would go if enough of us go.”**

## Problem

Adult friendships often have plenty of communication but less in-person time. Work, relationships, travel, routines, and different schedules make casual plans harder to initiate. The coordination cost shows up in familiar ways:

- someone says “we should get drinks this week” and nobody turns it into a concrete plan;
- the person proposing the idea does not want to book or organize something for two people when it is only worth doing with four;
- friends wait to see who else is going before committing;
- early public RSVPs create social pressure and anchoring;
- a plan that never reached enough interest still requires someone to formally cancel it.

The product problem is therefore not simply **finding a time**. It is deciding whether enough independent interest exists to make a casual plan real.

## Target user

### Primary
Adults in their 20s and 30s who have an established friend group but increasingly fragmented schedules. They already coordinate through group texts, Instagram DMs, WhatsApp, iMessage, or calendars.

### Initial use cases

- drinks tonight if 4 people are free;
- brunch tomorrow if 5 people want to go;
- a beach day if 6 people commit by Friday night;
- a pickup game if 8 players are in;
- a movie, dinner, museum, hike, board-game night, or day trip that only makes sense above a minimum group size.

## Job to be Done

> When I have an idea for something I would enjoy doing with friends, but it is only worth the effort if enough people join, help me test real commitment without becoming the organizer of a plan that may never happen.

Participant-side job:

> When a friend suggests something casual, let me commit honestly without needing to know who else said yes first, and tell me clearly when the plan becomes real.

## Value proposition

### For the person proposing the idea
- You can float an idea without promising to organize it regardless of turnout.
- You define what “enough” means before responses arrive.
- Confirmation happens automatically at the threshold.
- If quorum never happens, there is no manual cancellation ceremony.

### For friends
- Your vote is private during the socially sensitive phase.
- You do not have to perform enthusiasm publicly to help a plan get momentum.
- Once the plan is real, you get the information that becomes useful: who is going, where, and when.

## Product principles

### 1. An idea is not an event yet
The UI should use language such as “plan,” “idea,” “unlock,” and “if it happens” before quorum. This reduces the psychological cost of proposing something.

### 2. Private commitment before social proof
Before quorum, show the aggregate yes count but not participant identities. A person should answer based on whether they would go if the threshold is reached, not based on who has already committed.

### 3. Automatic means automatic
Once the threshold is hit, the plan confirms without the host needing to make a second decision. The threshold is the contract.

### 4. Reveal when identities become useful
After confirmation, reveal the guest list. At that point, coordination and anticipation matter more than protecting the first-mover decision.

### 5. Optimize for casual speed
Tonight/tomorrow/weekend presets, one-link sharing, no public feed, and no required group creation in v1.

### 6. Do not gamify reliability
No “flaky friend” scores, attendance grades, social ranking, or permanent history of declines. The product should reduce pressure, not weaponize it.

## Core hypothesis

If group members can commit privately to a threshold-based plan, then more casual ideas will reach a clear yes/no outcome with less coordination work than the same ideas handled through a group chat.

The hypothesis has three parts that must be measured separately:

1. **Clarity:** people reach a confirmed-or-not-confirmed outcome faster.
2. **Participation:** more invitees respond because the action is simple and private.
3. **Real-world value:** confirmed plans actually happen, rather than merely generating clicks.

## Non-goals for v1

- becoming a general-purpose calendar;
- replacing group chat;
- discovering strangers or public events;
- restaurant booking or ticket purchasing;
- algorithmic popularity feeds;
- AI-generated “things to do” as the primary value proposition;
- friend reliability scores;
- complex multi-date scheduling polls.

These may be useful later, but none should obscure the core question: **does blind quorum make spontaneous plans easier to turn into real time together?**
